Hypertension

Hypertension, also known as high blood pressure, is a condition in which the pressure inside the blood vessels remains consistently high. It is one of the leading causes of premature death worldwide and often develops without noticeable symptoms.

Overview

Blood pressure is measured using two numbers:
- Systolic pressure: the pressure when the heart contracts.
- Diastolic pressure: the pressure when the heart relaxes between beats.

Hypertension is generally diagnosed when blood pressure readings are consistently 140/90 mmHg or higher on two separate occasions.

Many people with hypertension are unaware they have the condition because symptoms are often absent. Regular blood pressure monitoring is essential for early detection.

Risk Factors

Several factors increase the risk of developing hypertension.

Modifiable risk factors include:
- High salt intake
- Diets rich in saturated or trans fats
- Low consumption of fruits and vegetables
- Physical inactivity
- Smoking
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Overweight or obesity
- Air pollution

Non-modifiable risk factors include:
- Family history of hypertension
- Age over 65 years
- Diabetes
- Chronic kidney disease

Symptoms

Most individuals with hypertension experience no symptoms.

Very high blood pressure may cause:
- Severe headache
- Chest pain
- Blurred vision
- Dizziness
- Difficulty breathing
- Nausea or vomiting
- Nosebleeds
- Anxiety
- Confusion
- Irregular heartbeat

People experiencing these symptoms together with very high blood pressure should seek immediate medical attention.

Diagnosis

Hypertension is diagnosed by measuring blood pressure on different occasions. Healthcare professionals evaluate blood pressure levels along with other medical conditions to determine appropriate treatment.

Treatment

Treatment aims to reduce blood pressure and lower the risk of complications.

Lifestyle changes include:
- Eating a healthy, low-salt diet
- Regular physical activity
- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Quitting tobacco
- Limiting alcohol consumption

When lifestyle changes are not enough, medications may be prescribed.

Common medication classes include:
- ACE inhibitors
- Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs)
- Calcium channel blockers
- Diuretics

Healthcare providers determine the most appropriate treatment based on the patient's health status.

Prevention

Healthy lifestyle habits can help prevent hypertension and improve blood pressure control.

Recommendations include:
- Eating more fruits and vegetables
- Exercising regularly
- Reducing salt intake
- Managing stress
- Maintaining a healthy body weight
- Monitoring blood pressure regularly
- Following prescribed treatment plans

Complications

Uncontrolled hypertension can lead to serious health problems, including:
- Heart attack
- Heart failure
- Stroke
- Kidney disease
- Damage to blood vessels
- Irregular heart rhythm

WHO Response

The World Health Organization (WHO) supports countries through evidence-based guidelines, the Global Hearts Initiative, and programs that improve hypertension prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and long-term management worldwide.
