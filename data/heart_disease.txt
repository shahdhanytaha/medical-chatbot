Heart Disease

Heart disease is a general term that describes several conditions affecting the heart. One of the most common types is coronary artery disease (CAD), which reduces blood flow to the heart. Reduced blood flow can increase the risk of a heart attack.

Overview

Heart disease may develop without noticeable symptoms. In some cases, it is not diagnosed until a person experiences a serious event such as a heart attack, heart failure, or an abnormal heart rhythm.

Common Symptoms

Symptoms vary depending on the specific heart condition.

Heart Attack:
- Chest pain or discomfort
- Pain in the upper back or neck
- Heartburn
- Nausea or vomiting
- Extreme fatigue
- Dizziness
- Shortness of breath

Arrhythmia:
- Fluttering or irregular heartbeat (palpitations)

Heart Failure:
- Shortness of breath
- Fatigue
- Swelling of the feet, ankles, legs, abdomen, or neck veins

Risk Factors

Several factors increase the risk of developing heart disease, including:
- High blood pressure
- High blood cholesterol
- Smoking
- Diabetes
- Overweight or obesity
- Unhealthy diet
- Physical inactivity
- Excessive alcohol consumption

Treatment and Recovery

Recovery from heart disease may include participation in a cardiac rehabilitation program. Cardiac rehabilitation helps patients improve their health after a heart attack, heart failure, or certain heart surgeries.

A cardiac rehabilitation program may include:
- Regular physical activity
- Healthy eating education
- Taking medications as prescribed
- Smoking cessation support
- Stress management
- Mental health counseling

Healthcare professionals involved in cardiac rehabilitation may include:
- Physicians and nurses
- Exercise specialists
- Nutrition specialists
- Physical therapists
- Mental health professionals

Importance of Prevention

Maintaining a healthy lifestyle can help reduce the risk of heart disease. Regular exercise, a balanced diet, controlling blood pressure and cholesterol, avoiding tobacco, and limiting alcohol consumption are important preventive measures.
