Asthma

Asthma is a chronic respiratory disease that affects people of all ages. It occurs when the airways become inflamed and the muscles surrounding them tighten, making breathing more difficult. Asthma is one of the most common chronic diseases in children, but it can affect adults as well.

Overview

Asthma symptoms may range from mild to severe and can change over time. Although asthma cannot be cured, it can be effectively managed with appropriate treatment and regular medical care. Proper management allows many people with asthma to maintain a normal and active lifestyle.

Common Symptoms

People with asthma may experience one or more of the following symptoms:
- Persistent coughing, especially at night
- Wheezing during breathing
- Shortness of breath
- Difficulty breathing
- Chest tightness

Symptoms may become worse during exercise, at night, or during an asthma attack.

Common Triggers

Asthma symptoms can be triggered by:
- Dust
- Tobacco smoke
- Air pollution
- Pollen
- Animal fur or feathers
- Strong perfumes or cleaning products
- Cold weather
- Respiratory infections
- Physical exercise

Risk Factors

Several factors increase the likelihood of developing asthma, including:
- Family history of asthma
- Allergic conditions such as eczema or hay fever
- Exposure to tobacco smoke
- Air pollution
- Premature birth or low birth weight
- Obesity
- Exposure to chemicals or occupational dust

Complications

Poorly controlled asthma may lead to:
- Sleep disturbances
- Daytime fatigue
- Reduced concentration
- Missed school or work
- Emergency hospital visits
- Severe asthma attacks
- Death in severe untreated cases

Treatment

Asthma treatment mainly focuses on controlling symptoms and preventing attacks.

Common treatments include:
- Bronchodilator inhalers to open the airways
- Steroid inhalers to reduce airway inflammation
- Combination inhalers containing both medications

Some patients may require daily inhaler use depending on symptom severity. Spacer devices can improve medication delivery, especially for children.

Self-Care

People with asthma can improve symptom control by:
- Taking medications as prescribed
- Avoiding known triggers
- Following an asthma action plan
- Monitoring symptoms regularly
- Seeking medical attention when symptoms worsen

WHO Response

The World Health Organization (WHO) works to improve asthma diagnosis, treatment, and monitoring worldwide. WHO also promotes access to essential asthma medications, supports healthcare workers through clinical guidelines, and encourages strategies to reduce tobacco smoke exposure and air pollution.
